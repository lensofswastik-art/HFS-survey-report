Reliable AI at scale requires trust engineering, not legacy QE

AI is in production faster than enterprises can prove it’s safe, fair or accountable. HFS Research and QualiZeal asked 101 enterprise leaders where the gaps are.

    Finding 01 / 05

    AI is in production. The controls are still in pilots.

    57% run GenAI in production, but only 31% have a formal validation framework there.

    What the data shows

    • 93% run GenAI in some form; 20% already at scale.
    • 28% haven’t started a validation framework; 42% are piloting.
    • 31% haven’t started a dedicated AI quality team.

    Our take

    AI governance cannot outpace deployment. Tests should ship with every model and keep running: behavior checks, drift alerts and audit-ready evidence.

    Finding 02 / 05

    37% run AI with no one holding the line

    Ownership is spread across teams or missing entirely. QE owns AI quality in only 15%.

    What the data shows

    • 22% let the team that built the model validate it.
    • 12% hand it to risk and compliance, who can’t run the tests.
    • 15% give it to QE, the function built to test.

    Our take

    Ownership is the first control. A clear, named owner with go/no-go decision authority closes the accountability gap before AI moves into production.

    Finding 03 / 05

    Most see AI testing as different. Only 15% have built for it.

    Probabilistic AI is still tested with tools made for deterministic software.

    What the data shows

    • 52% use frameworks built for deterministic software.
    • 73% still rely on manual review of samples.
    • 34% see the problem but haven’t acted on it.

    Our take

    Prioritize purpose-built testing and critical trust attributes—bias, explainability, and regulatory evidence over generic accuracy evidence.

    Finding 04 / 05

    88% measure accuracy. 35% can evidence compliance.

    Enterprises can show their AI is accurate. Far fewer can show it’s fair, explainable or compliant.

    What the data shows

    • An 88%-accurate model can still fail a protected group.
    • 63% can’t explain an AI decision to a regulator.
    • Only 35% can produce compliance evidence today.

    Our take

    Swap the accuracy dashboard for a trust scorecard: fairness, explainability, robustness and evidence mapped to the NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act.

    Finding 05 / 05

    QE got the mandate. Only 24% got the budget.

    QE took on AI validation, data quality and security. Budgets mostly didn’t follow.

    What the data shows

    • 65% of QE teams now own AI model validation.
    • 48% got some extra resource, but not enough.
    • For 6%, budget or headcount shrank as scope grew.

    Our take

    Fund the QE mandate to match its extended scope with matching resources.

    Enterprise AI has already crossed the production threshold. With GenAI systems live in more than half of organizations, and agentic systems with real autonomy deployed in over a third of surveyed companies, quality and governance remain largely stuck in pilot mode.

    PG
    Pradeep GovindasamyPresident & CEO, QualiZeal

    Closing this gap requires treating AI assurance as a distinct discipline that combines deep Quality Engineering foundations with the platforms, methods, and continuous evaluation needed to demonstrate trust at scale.

    Recommendations

    What leaders should do next

    1. 01

      Appoint a named owner for AI quality

      One function, independent of the builders, that holds the line.

    2. 02

      Replace accuracy with a trust scorecard

      Measure safety, fairness, explainability and compliance evidence.

    3. 03

      Fund the QE mandate

      If QE covers AI validation, budget and headcount must keep pace.
